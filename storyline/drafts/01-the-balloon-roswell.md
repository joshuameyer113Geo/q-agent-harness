# VEILWAR — OPENING SCENE: THE BALLOON
# Draft 1 — 2026-10-27
# Era: Roswell, July 1947
# POV: Major Jesse Marcel, 509th Bomb Group intelligence officer
# Load-bearing lore: RAAF "flying disc" press release (Jul 8), next-day balloon re-identification, Project Mogul (1994), destroyed RAAF outgoing messages (1995 GAO). The first cover story is literally a balloon. That is the template.

---

## SCENE 1 — FOSTER RANCH, LINCOLN COUNTY, NEW MEXICO. NIGHT OF JULY 4, 1947.

The desert at night does not go quiet. It goes *attentive*.

Jesse Marcel stands at the edge of a pasture with a flashlight in one hand and a clipboard he has not written on in an hour. The wind moves through the scrub like something reading a list. Forty miles behind him, the 509th sits at Roswell Army Air Field — the only atomic bomb group in the world, the unit that ended a war, the unit that now spends its nights pretending to be a weather station.

The rancher, Mac Brazel, found it at dawn. Not a plane. Not a meteor. A field of debris scattered across three-quarters of a mile: foil that would not crease, sticks that would not burn, symbols that were not letters.

Marcel kneels and picks up a piece the size of a playing card. It is thin as cigarette paper and bright as a mirror, and when he folds it, it springs back flat without a crease line. He has handled radar targets before. This is not a radar target.

He writes one line on the clipboard: *NOT OURS.*

---

## SCENE 2 — RAAF HEADQUARTERS. MORNING OF JULY 8, 1947.

The press release goes out at 11:00. Four sentences. The 509th has in its possession a flying disc, recovered on a ranch in the Roswell vicinity.

By noon, the wires are burning. By 18:00, the story has a name in every paper in the country.

By 20:00, the story has a correction.

Eighth Air Force issues a new statement: the object was a radar-target weather balloon. A training device. Nothing more.

Marcel reads it twice. He was the one who collected the debris. He was the one who said *not ours*. He knows what a radar target looks like — balsa, foil, string, a corner reflector. What he held in that field did not behave like any of it.

He does not argue. He files the report, boxes the debris, and watches it go into a staff car bound for Fort Worth. The car leaves at 21:40. He never sees it again.

---

## SCENE 3 — THE MISSING PAPER. 1995.

Forty-eight years later, a General Accounting Office investigator whose name does not matter is searching the RAAF administrative files for 1946 through 1949. The office has been asked, by Congress, to find out what really happened at Roswell.

The outgoing messages are gone.

Not classified. Not redacted. *Gone.* Destroyed, per the base's own records, as routine. The only surviving documents are a unit history and an FBI teletype that says, in effect: we have no idea what this was, but we are not saying that out loud.

The investigator writes a sentence that ends up in a report nobody reads: the absence of records is itself information.

---

## SCENE 4 — THE TEMPLATE. PRESENT DAY. HUMAN AI CLASSIFICATION LAYER.

A sensor array over the Pacific picks up a return that falls like snow from above the scan volume. No wings. No exhaust. No lock.

The AI runs it against forty thousand prior cases. Balloons: 31,204. Birds: 4,891. Drones: 2,113. Clutter: 1,902.

Unexplained: 890.

The AI does what it was built to do. It does not say *alien*. It says *unresolved*. Because that is the honest word, and because the first cover story in the human record was literally a balloon — and the balloon was real, and the debris was not, and both things were true at the same time, in the same field, in the same week.

That is the template. Every deception since has been a variation on it.

The mundane explanation is often true for *some* cases. That is what makes it work.
