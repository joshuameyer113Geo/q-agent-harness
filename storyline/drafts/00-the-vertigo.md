# Scene Zero: The Vertigo

*This page sits between nothing and everything. Read it once, slowly, before scene one. Do not rush it. It is not a scene. It is the feeling underneath every scene.*

---

There is a moment — you know the one — when something large happens and your mind cannot decide if it is real.

You are standing in a kitchen. The coffee is cold. Your sister is still talking about something that mattered an hour ago and matters less now. And then the floor tilts, not physically, but in the way a floor tilts when you realize you have been standing on the wrong assumption your whole life.

Is this happening?

Am I going to wake up?

The mind does what minds do. It reaches. It grabs for the nearest frame — a movie you saw, a story your uncle told, a headline from the paper. Anything with a shape. Anything that says *this has a category, this belongs somewhere.*

But sometimes nothing fits.

Sometimes the thing that arrives has no creature in it, no ship, no invasion. Just lightweight foil that springs back flat when you crumple it. Just a window that pulls at you before you know why. Just a knock at the door at 5:40 in the morning, three polite taps, and a man outside holding something that should not exist and does.

The stories you grew up with were about Martians with green skin and ray guns. They were about invasion and escape and the hero who saves the day. Nobody wrote about the hour after. Nobody wrote about the cold coffee going colder while the extraordinary sits on the table and everyone pretends it is ordinary because pretending is the only move left.

That is where this book lives.

Not in the headlines. Not in the press release. Not in the correction that came out the same night.

In the seam between *is this real* and *I must be dreaming.* In the wobble. In the window.

Jesse Jr. stood at that window before anything happened. He did not know anything. He had no vocabulary for what was coming. He had only the pull — the mind reaching for a frame that did not exist yet.

That is the most honest thing in this entire story. Not the debris. Not the cover-up. The kid at the glass, feeling something arrive before the world had a word for it.

Hold that feeling. Carry it into scene one. Do not let go.

---

*End of scene zero.*
